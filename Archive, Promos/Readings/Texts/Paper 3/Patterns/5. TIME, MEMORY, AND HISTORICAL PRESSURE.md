
# PATTERN V: TIME, MEMORY, AND HISTORICAL PRESSURE  
## (Progress, Repetition, and Haunting)

---

## OVERARCHING FRAME

- Victorian culture is structured by belief in progress.  
- Literature both sustains and destabilises this belief.  
- Time becomes uneven, recursive, and haunted.

==> History does not move cleanly forward.

---

## REALISM (Time as Development)

- Realist narratives often track growth, reform, and maturation.  
- Time is presented as meaningful sequence.

### Core primary texts
- **Middlemarch**  
- **North and South**

---

## INTERRUPTION AND REPETITION

- Industrial modernity fragments time.  
- Memory interrupts narrative progress.  
- The past persists.

---

## GHOST STORIES (Time as Recurrence)

- Ghosts replay rather than resolve the past.  
- The present is haunted by unfinished histories.

### Core primary texts
- **Braddon, “At Chrighton Abbey”**

---

## SLAVERY AND HISTORICAL AFTERLIVES

- Slavery persists beyond emancipation.  
- History survives as memory, song, and mourning.

### Core primary texts
- **Du Bois, *Souls of Black Folk***  
- **Douglass**

---

## FIN DE SIÈCLE (Time as Exhaustion)

- Decadence figures history as decline.  
- Progress becomes irony.

### Core primary texts
- **Wilde, *Dorian Gray***  
- **Symons**

---

## SYNTHETIC INSIGHT

- Victorian literature does not abandon history.  
- It reimagines time as pressure rather than progress.

==> The past is never past.
