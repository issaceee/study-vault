>Ezra Pound’s In a Station of the Metro & Weak Modernism
>
>Saint-Armour posits that rather than totalising truth-claim, modernism should be understood as epistemic weakness, modest claims, fragmentary claims and meanings that are local and provisional. Pound’s poem captures only a singular moment in time, rather than a claim about the metro as a whole. Meaning in the poem is extremely subjective and interpretively unstable, with the diction of “apparition” serving to highlight a sense of ‘muddiness’ and instability. Moreover, the syntax of the one sentence that encompasses the entire poem also seems incomplete – what listeners get is only a fragment of a sentence, or in this case, a fragment of an experience of an instance of time. There is no speaker asserting authority in the poem, and no truth-claims made about the metro – in this poem, the metro simply _is_. It exists to be experienced, and the deliberate compression of form in the lyrical form serves as a sort of trust in vagueness and experience to capture this fleeting moment in time.
>
>As Saint-Armour posits, “It is content to let modernism be definitionally and constitutively vague” (452). The poem doesn’t seem to be making an argument, or even trying to make a claim, it seems content to just let the moment be; thereby exemplifying Saint-Armour’s weak modernism by refusing to totalise modern experience, offering a localised experience where meaning remains provisional and unresolved.
